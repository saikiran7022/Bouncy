1 The Big Picture

Being in the stock market comes in three "speeds." You're learning the middle one.

StyleHow long you holdScreen time
Day tradingminutes–hoursAll day, stressful
Swing trading ⬅️ youdays–weeksA few minutes a day
Investingmonths–yearsBarely any

Swing trading means you try to catch one "swing" — one clean leg of a move up. Think of price like a staircase: it steps up, rests, steps up again. You hop on for a step or two, then hop off.

💡 Why it fits you: you do your homework in the evening, place orders that run on autopilot, and glance once during the day. No screen-staring required.

Here's the secret: every trade is just 5 questions. Your entire strategy document is these five, dressed up. Learn them and you've learned trading.

3 Trend & Relative Strength — what to buy

Rule #1: only buy stocks going UP. Beginners love buying cheap, falling stocks hoping for a bounce. Don't — swim with the current. An uptrend = higher highs & higher lows, price above a rising average line.

Relative Strength: buy the leaders

Relative strength just means: is this stock stronger than the overall market? When the market wobbles, the strong stocks barely dip — because big money wants them. 5 The Stop-Loss — where you're wrong

The most important lesson here. Before you buy, you pick the price where you'll admit it failed and get out. You decide it while calm, not while panicking.

It goes just below the setup's key level — the spot that, if broken, kills your reason for buying:

SetupStop goes just below…
Pullbackthe low of the dip
Basethe bottom of the box
Higher lowthat higher low

Real example — AMD: entry ≈ $522, support ≈ $507, so the stop sits at $505. If AMD trades $505, the setup failed → you're out, no debate.

⚙️ Make it automatic: on Robinhood, place a stop order at $505 the moment you buy. Now you're protected even while you're at work.

🚫 Iron rule: never move a stop down to give a loser "more room." That's how a small loss becomes a disaster. You may only trail it up to lock in profit.

6 Reward & "R" — why 2:1 is everything

Meet "R", your most useful word. 1R = the amount you risk on a trade. For you that's about $50 (1% of $5,000). Your target should be worth at least 2R — twice what you risk.

AMD: risk = $522 − $505 = $17/share (1R)  →  target $580 = +$58 = ~3.4R ✅

The math that proves you don't need to be right often

💰 The whole edge: make your winners bigger than your losers (≥2:1) and the math carries you. Chasers do the opposite — tiny wins, big losses — and lose even when "right" most days. Where's the target? Usually the next obvious level: a prior high, round number, or "measured move."

7 Position Sizing — how many shares

You never pick share count by gut. You calculate it from your risk:

Shares = (dollars you'll risk) ÷ (entry price − stop price)

On a small account you have two caps — and you always use whichever gives you fewer shares:

  • Risk cap: never risk more than 1R ($50).
  • Concentration cap: never put more than ~15% of the account in one stock.

For pricey stocks like AMD ($500+), the concentration cap usually wins, so you'll quietly risk less than 1% — that's good. Fractional shares (Robinhood has them) let you buy an exact dollar amount. Try it 👇

🧮 Change the numbers — try a $50 stock with a $46 stop to see the risk cap win, then AMD ($522 / $505) to see the concentration cap win. Cheaper stocks ($30–150) usually let your full 1% risk work and are easier to size.

9 Mindset & Your Game Plan

This is what actually decides if you win. The mechanics are easy; following them is hard.

The 5 habits that keep beginners alive

  • Risk the same small amount every time (your 1R ≈ $50). Never "bet big" because you "feel sure."
  • Always use a stop, always set it first. No exceptions.
  • Take the trade only if it scores ≥7/10. Skipping bad trades IS a winning move — cash is a position.
  • Don't revenge-trade. After a loss, the market doesn't owe you anything. Walk away.
  • Keep a journal: screenshot the chart, write why you bought, your stop/target, and later — what happened. This is how you actually improve.

🚦 Your starter routine (fits your ~hourly schedule)

  1. Evening (10 min): check the market mood (SPY/QQQ up? VIX calm under 20?). Scan 1–2 leaders for a clean setup.
  2. Write the plan: entry, stop, target, size — using the calculator in Lesson 7.
  3. Place orders that run themselves: a limit buy at your pullback price + a stop-loss. Done.
  4. One glance midday. Otherwise, let it work.
🎓 Most important next step: practice with FAKE money first. "Paper trade" 10–20 setups — write the full plan, track results — before risking a real dollar. You'll learn the same lessons for free.
